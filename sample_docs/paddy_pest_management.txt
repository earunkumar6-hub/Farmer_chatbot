Paddy (Rice) Pest Management — Field Reference Note

Stem Borer
- Symptom: "dead heart" in the vegetative stage (central shoot dries up) and
  "white ear" during the reproductive stage (empty, whitish panicle).
- Cultural control: remove and destroy stubble after harvest, since larvae
  survive in stubble; avoid staggered planting in the same block, which lets
  the pest cycle continuously between fields.
- Biological control: conserve natural enemies such as Trichogramma egg
  parasitoids; avoid unnecessary broad-spectrum insecticide sprays early in
  the season, as these kill natural enemies along with the pest.
- Monitoring: install pheromone traps to track adult moth activity and time
  interventions instead of spraying on a fixed calendar.

Brown Planthopper (BPH)
- Symptom: yellowing and drying of plants in patches, often starting at the
  base and spreading outward — sometimes called "hopper burn."
- Favorable conditions: dense planting, excess nitrogen fertilizer, and
  continuous flooding all favor BPH buildup.
- Management: avoid excess nitrogen; maintain alternate wetting and drying
  instead of continuous standing water where feasible; encourage predators
  like spiders and mirid bugs by minimizing insecticide use unless the
  population crosses the locally recommended threshold.

Leaf Folder
- Symptom: leaves folded longitudinally with the larva feeding inside,
  leaving visible white streaks.
- Management: this pest is usually tolerated up to a moderate level of leaf
  damage without yield loss, since rice compensates well; intervention is
  generally only needed if folding is severe and sustained across the crop
  canopy.

General integrated pest management (IPM) principles for paddy:
- Use resistant or tolerant varieties recommended for the local agro-climatic
  zone where available.
- Follow synchronized planting within a block to break the pest life cycle.
- Scout the field regularly (at least weekly) rather than spraying on a fixed
  schedule.
- Use chemical control only when pest levels cross the Economic Threshold
  Level (ETL) recommended for that pest and region, and always follow the
  label dose and pre-harvest interval.

For dosage-specific chemical recommendations, consult the current state
agriculture department advisory or the local Krishi Vigyan Kendra, since
approved molecules and doses are updated periodically.

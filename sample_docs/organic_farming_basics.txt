Organic Farming Basics — Field Reference Note

What organic farming avoids
- Synthetic chemical fertilizers and synthetic pesticides.
- Genetically modified seed material, under most certification standards.
- Growth-promoting synthetic hormones in livestock integrated with the farm.

Soil fertility building blocks
- Farmyard manure (FYM): well-rotted cattle manure improves soil organic
  matter and slow-releases nutrients.
- Vermicompost: compost produced using earthworms, generally richer in
  available nutrients than raw FYM and quicker to prepare.
- Green manuring: growing a fast-cycle legume crop like dhaincha or sunhemp
  and ploughing it into the soil before flowering to add nitrogen and organic
  matter.
- Panchagavya and Jeevamrutha: traditional liquid formulations made from cow
  dung, cow urine, and other locally available inputs, used as a soil and
  plant tonic in many Indian organic systems.

Pest and disease management without synthetic chemicals
- Neem-based formulations (neem oil, neem seed kernel extract) act as a
  repellent and growth disruptor for several insect pests.
- Trap crops and border crops (such as marigold around vegetable plots) can
  draw pests away from the main crop.
- Encouraging natural predators by maintaining some flowering border
  vegetation supports pollinators and beneficial insects.

Certification basics
- Organic certification in India is typically governed under the National
  Programme for Organic Production (NPOP) framework, with a conversion
  period required before land can be certified organic.
- Farmer groups can also use Participatory Guarantee System (PGS) certification,
  which is a peer-review based system generally intended for local and
  domestic markets rather than export.

Common challenges
- Yield may dip in the first few seasons of transition before soil biology
  and fertility rebuild.
- Organic inputs need advance planning and preparation time (e.g., compost
  takes weeks to mature), unlike ready-to-use synthetic fertilizers.

For certification procedures and current subsidy schemes for organic inputs,
farmers should check with the state horticulture or agriculture department,
as programs and subsidy amounts are revised periodically.
